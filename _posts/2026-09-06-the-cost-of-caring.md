I was inspired to write this after reading some LinkedIn posts from a prominent CEO here in Dallas. Every post is transparently written by Claude. There is no attempt by this CEO's handlers to tweak the content to make it less sloppy. The actual content is often good, but the actual "writing" has that slop stink and makes it indigestible. 

Then the two links above crossed my desk.

### Caring

My working hypothesis for this slop age we're living in is this: these people never cared. I don't have the expectation that everyone on the internet care about what they publish there, lord knows that ship has sailed. Online marketers haven't cared about their craft since the first time someone breathed "SEO".

But I have assumed that executives and other professionals representing their intellectual abilities on the internet would care. I now think that assumption was wrong all along.

But they care about _something_, right? Otherwise why post?

Over a year ago [I said](/2025/05/17/general-audience-ai-presentation/):

> LLMs aren’t good writers if you’re trying to actually sound smart or original. Conversely, LLMs are good at navigating scenarios where business theater has replaced actual truth and meaning, like security audits. LLMs are great bullshit machines.

### Capital, Again?

We're in an age where much of the labor required by capital is [bullshit work](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bullshit_Jobs "Link to the Wikipedia article for the book 'Bullshit Jobs', which I have not read, FWIW"). So much of the work required to maintain software has been [pushed onto the customers of that software](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enshittification "Link to the Wikipedia article for 'Enshittification', which does not exactly match what I am saying here but it's in the ballpark"). I don't need to review that landscape. But that simple observation brings us back to LinkedIn and CEOs' Claude-generated posts.

Business leaders are, and have always been, half business and half theater. Some excel at both (Steve Jobs comes to mind). Some lean more towards one or the other. But neither necessarily rewards creative craft. Both modes are simply checkpoints on the way to greater profitability.

So if you feel obligated to post to LinkedIn to fulfill your duties, but don't have anything to say, don't have the time to say something you thought and wrote yourself, or simply don't care about writing and posting...well, Claude to the rescue.

There is a lot of bullshit in my life that I'd love to outsource to Claude. Despite my best efforts, the level of bullshit has only grown since I observed it and began attempts at fighting it. AI promises to remove that bullshit, yet somehow..._unbelievably, I know_...has only multiplied the stinky piles.
