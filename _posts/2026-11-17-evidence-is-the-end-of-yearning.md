> ...we cling to any intimations of the divine – those strange, uncertain spectral activities – no matter how absurd or sentimental they may be, because the idea of an indifferent universe can feel impossible to bear. Our rational minds reduce such things to mere coincidence - balloons lose air, they land by chance - but some of us possess a yearning of an intensity that is qualitatively more than just wishful thinking. We understand this yearning as our primary and fundamental state, as the presence of God expressed precisely through his absence. We grow acutely sensitive to those small, often childlike signs that our logical minds want to reject, but our impractical, unreasonable hearts rush toward, gathering up the whispered hints of a divine presence, like celestial crumbs.  

> None of this is evidence, one way or another, but some of us aren’t looking for evidence – evidence is the end of yearning, that poetic force that enriches our lives so beautifully. We feel alive inside the longing, the mystery, the strangeness.

[Nick Cave - The Red Hand Files - Issue #382](https://www.theredhandfiles.com/strange-sorts-of-activity/)
