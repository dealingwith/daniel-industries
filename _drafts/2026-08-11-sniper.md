#### DH

If SFC (Ret.) Mitchell Justice was lucky, and he often was, they would come looking for him and he'd have to make a run for it. The reference plan if nothing went wrong was for him to shimmy back out the way he came in eleven hours later. But a forced exit would have him back home potentially before lunch. All things being equal, that sounded better.

Justice had lain still for as much as two days in places much worse than this. The sun was not too warm. The grass smelled properly grassy. There weren't even really any bugs. He'd take it.

He was comfortable. He was invisible. His scope was tight on the front door of an old, wooden bait-and-tackle shop. Nothing to do now but wait.

He noticed a dead squirrel about two feet from his nest. It was gray-black and didn't smell. Long dead, he guessed.

The building looked like it would collapse in a strong breeze. A neon Miller Lite sign in the window fritzed in the window. An old dog lay sleeping by the door.

He really hoped the dog would stay out of it.

Today's target was one Albrecht Amadeus Konstantin. Justice knew nothing about the man except that he drove a black BMW and was expected out this way on a fishing trip. And that he traveled with an armed escort. Apparently Herr Konstantin routinely stopped at this shack for worms and beer on the way. Time would tell.

A car pulled up. Not the black BMW but a red, nearly totaled Corolla, tinted windows peeling, bass rattling every loose part, of which there were many. When the door finally opened, out of a haze of dank smoke, stepped the largest man Justice had ever seen. His too-small t-shirt read, "Uber Eats."

He was, indeed, above eats. He had conquered eating. His movement could only be described as lumbering, and he struggled to extract the insulated bag from the backseat. Like a giant penguin he waddled up the rickety steps, pulled a large paper bag out from within the larger insulated square, placed it in front of the door--noticed with only a passing curiosity by the dog, who barely turned his head--and took a photo of the deposit with his cellphone.

As he wobbled back to his Toyota, the front door opened and a gaunt, silver-haired man in overalls and a yellowed t-shirt emerged from the bait shop, and as he stooped to retrieve his meal, waved and shouted, "Thanks, Stanley!"

Stanley turned half-way, now diagonally waddling as he also waved high and said, "Have a good one, George!" He got back to the driver's side, chucked the bag into the backseat through the open window, and squeezed into the car like a slime ghost traveling a city's water infrastructure.

That was when a black BMW practically flew into Justice's scope-sized view, skidding to a stop in the gravel in front of the shop. Even from his vantage point, he could hear the fine rocks ping off Stanley's car. He could see him try to duck from inside the car.

Two men, dressed in black fashion-forward tactical gear, emerged from both rear doors. The windows were a solid black, Justice couldn't see any other passengers inside the vehicle, not even through the doors for the few seconds they were open. The men moved quickly towards the door, and Justice noticed Stanley and George both squint in confusion at the men. They pushed by George and his plastic bag of General Tso's chicken into the store.

#### DH

The unfortunate truth was this was Mitchell Justice's first "private" gig. Private meaning extrajudicial. Like, hit man. Like, assassin.

But, he had the skills and needed the money. He figured if he had any moral qualms about the whole thing he'd sort it out if the time ever came.

Well, the time came. In fact, he did not have it sorted out at all. Too late.

He needed Konstantin to show his face. Justice was not about to shoot blind into the back of the car. Clock was ticking.

Sight trained on the BMW, he mentally rehearsed.

One shot. Target goes down. Wait. Lie still. Watch. All day if need be. Anyone starts walking intently this direction, ditch the ghillie and the gear and run for the car, side arm only.

The driver's side rear door of the BMW opened and Justice did a double take. An enthusiastically flexible young woman wearing nothing but a beach coverup and flip flops executed some kind of ballet maneuver to depart the vehicle and danced slowly toward the shop, arms spread, hair crazed, eyes near closed, twirling and humming.

Things moved quickly. Ladyfriend spies dog, runs over, bends at hips. "Oooooh! You are so suh-weeeeet! Awww!" Dog props up, begins making out with special lady. Stanley loses all motor control staring at the scene. George lurches to grab dog. One of the goons shouts something at George. Driver gets out of vehicle to run after ladyfriend.

And then, there he was. Bald, short, irritated Al Konstantin, gesticulating with a cigarello and whispering hotly into his phone. He stepped out of the car and walked ten paces into the grass. He looked almost dead into Justice's scope as he undid his fly with his non-phone hand and prepared to take a leak.

"Shit. Shit shit shit shit," Justice whispered into the rifle just below the scope. "Have I ever shot someone with their dick in their hand?" Memories of a hundred shots displayed as rotoscoped prints throughout a mind palace adorned with Roman columns. He realized that the majority of those were at night, the figure just an outline, a blob. The sun shone brightly down on this target, its clothes a body-shaped black hole among the bright grasses, the bright gravel, the edges of the bright Toyota now partly obscured. But mostly the bright spot of his head, the phone held tight, the smoke held between chattering lips. He'd left his stream machine propped over the front of his pants so he could continue gesticulating to whoever was on the other side of the phone call. It was, frankly, mesmerizing. Finally, he took the cig out of his mouth and flicked it into the tall, dry grasses in front of him, pinched the phone between his shoulder and his ear, freeing both hands to lift his pants back over his penis, rearrange it rather brutishly, re-tuck the front of his shirt, and zip. He turned as his hand returned to his phone, his head straightened, and his talking-with-his-hands arm returned to its animations.

"Fuck it," Justice said, and he began that slow squeeze that was all muscle memory. Time slowed, slower than seemed possible. It was familiar now. The breath had stopped. Millimeters of travel in seconds that felt like minutes.

There must have been one micron left in his trigger movement when Al suddenly fell. The trigger finger immediately released, while there was still no thinking. Justice snapped back to real time. Al's face slammed into the grass and gravel mix, his arms failing to catch him.

#### DH

Heart attack? Stroke? Alien beam weapon? Justice didn't know and didn't particularly care. While the unfolding pandemonium in his scope reached new levels of unbelievability, he had to mentally take a step back and take stock.

Certainly, he'd have to wait around for a while to see whether Albrecht was actually dead. He sure looked dead... Justice thought about how much it would suck to be just barely resuscitated by EMS only to find oneself shot on the gurney. He really hoped it didn't go that way.

Given there was no gunshot, nobody would be looking for him, so he could wait this out all day if he had to.

Then there was the awkward question of whether to confirm and/or take credit for the kill. His bosses very likely did not care how Konstantin wound up dead, just that he got there. But any inconsistency in his side of the story would reflect badly if there was to be follow-on work. He was honestly not sure he wanted any such. He'd probably just tell the truth and risk not getting paid.

The grass under and around Justice gave a weird, dry crackle as he adjusted his position. Odd, this patch was lush when he'd first set down.

By now, the driver, the no-neck brutes, and the special ladyfriend were all huddled around the man, with Stanley and George hovering a few steps back and craning their necks. The dog resumed sleeping.

"Sweetie? Honey? Baby? What's wrong??" she was saying, over and over. One of the goons was on the phone giving a staccato SIT REP to someone who, presumably, understood the jargon.

"Hold on, you know what, though?" Justice thought to himself. "This is bullshit. All of it."

And then he had an idea.

#### DM

He did the math again in his head. The same math he did just about every day since he retired. If he could get a decent price on that piece of land in west Texas, cashed out his investments, and found an apartment for under one k a month, he could make it until his retirement pay kicked in. He could just walkaway, never report back in with his employer, or just send a message that he hadn't done the job in time, thanks for the opportunity, something, anything that would keep them off his tail.

The woman was trying to turn Albrecht over, and one of the goons helped enough to roll him onto his back. There was a tiny bit of blood where his lip and nose had collided with the gravel, but otherwise no sign of physical harm. Then, almost imperceptibly at first, his skin began to darken. Not redden, like during a cardiac arrest. A gray, creeping up his neck, then filling his cheeks, then darkening slowly to a pitch black. It was the uncanniest thing Justice had ever seen, and he'd seen plenty.

Then the goon on the phone flopped to the ground in an instant.

Then Justice realized he was no longer up on his elbows. He was completely prone, next to his rifle. His vision clouded at the edges. He knew his eye was no longer on the scope, but he was still seeing through it. The woman collapsed. Then the second goon. Then the cloudy parts of his vision began to turn dark and close in from the sides. His body was getting cold. He knew his skin was turning dark as a starless night sky.

He thought, "I won't need to sell that land after all."
